          The complete rules

          Everything you need to play from scratch. These are the Version 1 rules, the default. The Naos rules are listed after them and can be switched on in Game settings.

          1Boards

          Seven boards sit one above another. From the bottom up: Sirius, Arcturus, Antares, Mira, Rigel, Deneb, Naos. Each board is 6 rows of 3 squares, nine dark and nine light. "Up" and "down" mean the neighbouring boards in that order.

          2Pieces

          Each player has 27 pieces in their colour. Every piece combines two of the three alchemical elements: Salt Mercury Sulphur

          The big symbol is the outer element and decides how the piece moves. The small badge is the inner element and doesn't affect movement. A Sulphur (Salt) piece moves like Sulphur.

          3Start

          Salt pieces start on Sirius (6) and Arcturus (3), Mercury pieces on Antares (6) and Rigel (3), and Sulphur pieces on Deneb (6) and Naos (3). Black stands on dark squares at the top of each board, White on light squares at the bottom. Mira starts empty. White moves first, then players alternate, moving one piece per turn.

          4How they move

          • Salt: one step diagonally to a free square on its own board, or to any free square of the same shade on the board directly above or below.
          • Mercury: to any free square on its own board, or on a board one or two levels up or down.
          • Sulphur: to any free square on any board.

          Pieces only land on free squares. Nothing blocks the way, because pieces jump straight to their destination.

          5Transformation

          After every move, the piece turns into the next one in the cycle and follows its new movement rule:

          Example: Salt (Sulphur) moves and becomes Mercury (Salt), so from now on it moves like Mercury.

          6Capture

          Only a Sulphur (Sulphur) standing on Naos can capture. Instead of moving, it removes any opposing piece on any board except Naos. The captured piece leaves the game, and the capturer stays where it is and becomes Salt (Salt). Capturing is optional.

          7Goal

          Each player has three goal squares on Mira, marked by coloured frames: White takes the top triangle, Black the bottom triangle. The first player to occupy all three of their squares wins.

          Colour only (default): any of your pieces counts. Exact pieces: specific phases are needed. White needs Salt (Mercury) top-left, Salt (Salt) top-right and Salt (Sulphur) in the middle. Black needs Salt (Salt) bottom-left, Salt (Mercury) bottom-right and Salt (Sulphur) in the middle. Because a piece changes every time it moves, you have to plan its route through the transformations.

          8Three moves on Mira

          A piece may stay on Mira for at most three of its owner's turns, counting the turn it arrived on. When its time is up, it must move on the next turn, either across Mira (which gives it two more turns) or off to another board. Until it moves, no other piece may move. If several pieces are due, move one per turn. So you can hold at most three pieces on Mira at a time, exactly enough for the pattern. Pieces that are due glow amber.

          9Skipping a turn

          If a player has no legal move at all (all their pieces are blocked, or a piece whose time on Mira is up has nowhere to go and no other piece may move), they skip the turn and the opponent moves straight away. Nothing needs to be pressed: the app skips automatically and writes "no moves, turn passes" in the log. As soon as a move becomes available, the player moves as usual.

          If neither player has any moves, the game ends in a draw.

          10Variants

          • No Mira limit: pieces may stay on Mira as long as they like. The limit can also be set to 5 or 7 moves.
          • No captures on Mira: a Sulphur (Sulphur) on Naos cannot capture pieces on Mira. This is the easiest version.

          Both switches are in Game settings.

          NNaos rules

          The original texts describe the game in two versions that disagree. Version 1 is above. The version printed in Naos changes these rules:

          • Salt moves only within its own board, to any free square.
          • Mercury moves to any free square on its own board or on the board directly above or below.
          • Sulphur moves as before, to any free square on any board.
          • Capture: a Sulphur (Sulphur) on any board can capture an opposing piece anywhere except on Naos. It stays where it is and becomes Salt (Salt).
          • Goal: the three pieces on your goal squares must be Salt pieces (Salt outer element).
          • Mira limit: the book also allows extending it to five or seven moves (Game settings → Time limit on Mira).

          Because Salt can't leave its board in this version, a Salt piece on Mira stays on Mira until it turns into Mercury and can step off.

          ?How to move in the app

          Tap your piece → red dots show where it can go → tap a dot. A ring around an opposing piece means you can capture it. "💡 Hint" picks a good move for you. Move highlighting can be switched off in Game settings, and then you work out the moves yourself. Drag the background to turn the boards.

          Background

          The Star Game comes from the "Septenary" occult tradition, where it is presented as both a strategy game and a symbolic system. Each board stands for one of seven spheres, and the three elements are the alchemical Salt, Mercury and Sulphur.
